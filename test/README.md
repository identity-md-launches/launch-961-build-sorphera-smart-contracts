# Sorphera adversarial tests

Run `forge build` and `forge test` with the existing configuration. No added packages, RPC URL, keys, environment mutation, or configuration edits are required.

`helpers/SorpheraFixture.sol` deploys the mocks, router, factory and lottery exactly as `Sorphera.t.sol` does, including the one-time `SorpheraVaultFactory.setLottery` binding that the revised implementation requires before `validateLaunch`.

`SorpheraAdversarial.t.sol` adds atomic rollback for invalid batches and payment errors; empty/oversized maintenance and claims; mixed-owner and duplicate claims; every one of the 5,700 valid combinations with all six permutations and collision checks; malformed/unknown VRF callbacks; active-rule snapshots; paused-sales settlement; an NFT receiver that owns the winning ticket and attempts a second claim during delivery; minimum-price cancellation and sub-ticket late recovery accumulation; and vault-only callers for `credit` and `recordNFT`, including one vault impersonating another round. The price/refund property runs 1,000 bounded examples, including the supported minimum/maximum through Foundry's bound mapping.

For the revised custody rules it also checks: the vault's ERC721 hook is callable by anyone but keys its receipt stamp by caller, so a stamp forged by a non-collection cannot backdate the real collection's late delivery; receipt exactly at the settlement deadline is late and cancels, while receipt one second earlier that is reconciled after the deadline draws (a 512-run property over receipt time and reconcile time); a stuck FWA delivery that is recovered during the next round of the same rollover group joins that round's inventory and belongs only to its winner, while the originating round's expired tickets cannot claim ETH or NFTs from their own vault; and a second `Sorphera` deployed against the already-bound factory cannot validate, open rounds or create vaults, nor can the owner rebind the factory.

`SorpheraInvariant.t.sol` uses three actors and independent games over bounded random sequences. Inline settings travel with the suite: 256 runs, depth 64, unexpected handler reverts fail the run. Its handler drives sales, blackout failures, acquisitions, allocations, settlements, refunds, vault donations and synchronization, separate VRF callbacks/finalization, up to four rounds per game, late claims, failed/retried NFT transfers, unauthorized withdrawals, pauses, purchaser epoch rewards, builder allowance claims, helper failures, queued-token delivery, and FWA resolving allocations the vault never settled: forced cashout, forced NFT delivery, and a delivery the collection rejects which FWA parks as stuck, later recovered through `recoverNFT`. Predetermined mock words deliberately reach matching and nonmatching tickets. The existing sampler fuzz tests cover arbitrary words.

The properties compare independently tracked incoming/outgoing ETH with held assets, reconcile liabilities against reserved fees/cash/unpaid ticket entitlements, check each game's conservation independently, account for purchaser and builder tokens separately including helper escrow, check pending-request counts, actual NFT custody, persistent terminal states/random words, and the fixed outcome rules: a cancelled round never holds a VRF request or releases fees, every drawn round with sales holds exactly one bound request and releases its held fees once, zero-ticket rounds never use VRF, NFT draws always freeze inventory, the winning NFT ticket is one of the indexed matching tickets, eligible inventory never exceeds the group's inventory, and every recorded asset has a receipt stamp no later than now. Ghost variables update only after successful calls. A deterministic handler lifecycle drives claims, retried deliveries, stuck recovery, forced cashout, a cancellation with a pending request, the late forced delivery into that cancelled round and the refund claims, so those paths are exercised without depending on random reachability.

All FWA, NFT, rewards, Permit2, helper, and lottery-coordinator behavior in these tests is mocked using the existing local models. The mock quote and reward conversion rate are harness inputs, not live protocol constants or revenue promises. ERC721 ownership checks assume compliant collections, and the receipt stamp assumes FWA's forced deliveries use `safeTransferFrom`, as `docs/OPERATIONS.md` documents. These tests do not establish deployed FWA compatibility, authentic Chainlink proofs, live settlement timing, market liquidity, or subscription funding. The existing `integration/Sepolia.t.sol` and `docs/DEPENDENCIES.md` describe the separate real-dependency checks and published Sepolia compatibility blocker; no new live fork run is claimed here.

The custody/deadline defect reported in the previous round (an NFT delivered before the settlement deadline but reconciled after it caused a wrong cancellation) was fixed by the implementation's receipt-time stamp; the former proof is now covered by `testCustodyBeforeDeadlineCountsEvenWhenReconciledAfter` in `Sorphera.t.sol` and by the boundary property above. No open finding remains from this round. No production implementation or protected configuration was changed.
